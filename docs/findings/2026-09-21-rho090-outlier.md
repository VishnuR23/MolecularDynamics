# Experiment 05 at rho* = 0.900: the simulation never melts

**Date:** 2026-09-21
**Experiments:** `experiments/exp05_lj_eos.sh`, `experiments/exp05b_rho090_diagnostic.sh`
**Data:** `results/exp05_lj_eos.csv`, `results/exp05b_rho090_diagnostic.csv`,
`results/exp05b_{msd,rdf}_rho0.900_seed{5006,5007,5008}.csv`,
`results/exp03_rdf_rahman.csv`
**Status:** open, diagnosed. This is a deliberate, documented failure:
`make reproduce` exits non-zero because of it, and that is correct.

## Summary

Experiment 05 compares our Lennard-Jones equation of state against NIST's
own tabulated MD isotherm at six densities. Five agree to better than
0.011 in U* and 0.040 in p*. The sixth, rho* = 0.900, does not: U* is too
negative by 0.390 and p* is too low by 2.452 — not a small discrepancy
but a different phase.

It is a different phase. At this state point our simulation does not
reach the liquid NIST tabulated. It stays in the face-centred-cubic
lattice it was started from. Three independent trajectories give a
self-diffusion coefficient of 1.5e-5, 2.9e-5 and 3.7e-5, against NIST's
tabulated 0.027 for the same (rho*, T*) — three orders of magnitude
lower, and indistinguishable from zero.

The engine is not wrong here. The protocol is: 50 t* of Langevin
equilibration at T* = 0.851 does not melt a perfect fcc lattice at this
density, and nothing in the driver forces it to.

## What experiment 05 measures

N = 500 (fcc, 5 cells/side — exactly NIST's N), rc* = 3.0, dt = 0.005,
50 t* Langevin equilibration followed by 100 t* NVT production
(Nosé-Hoover chain) pinned at each row's own NIST-quoted T*. Plain
truncation with the analytic tail correction on both energy and pressure,
which is NIST's "standard long range corrections" convention.

Deviations are `ours − NIST`. Tolerances, chosen a priori:
|dU*| <= 0.1, |dp*| <= 0.3, |dT*| <= 0.02.

| rho* | our T* | NIST T* | dT* | our U* | NIST U* | dU* | our p* | NIST p* | dp* | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.776 | 0.8481 | 0.851 | −0.0029 | −5.5136 | −5.517 | +0.0034 | −0.0094 | 0.030 | −0.0394 | PASS |
| 0.780 | 0.8525 | 0.853 | −0.0005 | −5.5354 | −5.533 | −0.0024 | +0.0583 | 0.072 | −0.0137 | PASS |
| 0.820 | 0.8511 | 0.852 | −0.0009 | −5.7925 | −5.803 | +0.0105 | +0.5553 | 0.573 | −0.0177 | PASS |
| 0.840 | 0.8483 | 0.851 | −0.0027 | −5.9148 | −5.909 | −0.0058 | +0.8786 | 0.910 | −0.0314 | PASS |
| 0.860 | 0.8525 | 0.849 | +0.0035 | −6.0272 | −6.027 | −0.0002 | +1.2840 | 1.282 | +0.0020 | PASS |
| 0.900 | 0.8492 | 0.851 | −0.0018 | −6.6239 | −6.234 | **−0.3899** | +0.0921 | 2.544 | **−2.4519** | **FAIL** |

Across the five passing rows the mean |dU*| is 0.0045 and the mean |dp*|
is 0.0209 — roughly 2% and 7% of their tolerances. The largest |dT*|
anywhere is 0.0035, confirming the thermostat really does land on NIST's
own temperature, so this is a like-for-like comparison and not a
different-state-point artefact. Against that background the rho* = 0.900
row is not a marginal miss; it is 3.9x its U* tolerance and 8.2x its p*
tolerance while its neighbour at rho* = 0.860 agrees to 0.0002.

## What the system actually is at rho* = 0.900

`exp05b_rho090_diagnostic.sh` runs the same state point with NVE
production and records g(r), the MSD and the VACF. It applies one
threshold, chosen a priori: `D_msd` must clear 0.005, a floor set well
below any liquid D measured anywhere in this project (experiment 04's
smallest is 0.031, NIST's own D* here is 0.027) and well above zero.
Clearing it means "diffusing, even if slowly"; failing it means "frozen".

Three independent seeds. **All three fail, by three orders of magnitude.**

| seed | D_msd | D_vacf | first g(r) peak | g at r* = 4.02 | |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5006 | 1.54e-5 | 1.09e-4 | 3.125 at r* = 1.098 | 1.102 | FAIL |
| 5007 | 2.91e-5 | 2.50e-4 | 3.121 at r* = 1.098 | 1.102 | FAIL |
| 5008 | 3.74e-5 | 3.26e-5 | 3.085 at r* = 1.098 | 1.102 | FAIL |

NIST's tabulated D* at this exact (rho*, T*) is 0.027. Ours is ~1000x
smaller. The three seeds agree with each other to within a few percent on
every structural quantity, which is itself the point: this is not a
marginal, trajectory-dependent outcome. It is what the protocol does.

Four independent signatures, all from the committed trajectories
(seed 5006 quoted; the other two agree):

**1. The MSD is flat.** It rises ballistically to 0.085 by t* = 1 and
then goes nowhere: 0.0793 at t* = 2.5, 0.0806 at t* = 5, 0.0805 at
t* = 10. Over t* in [1, 10] it stays inside 0.0790–0.0853. A liquid's MSD
grows linearly forever; this one is bounded, which is what atoms
vibrating about fixed lattice sites do.

**2. The vibration amplitude is exactly a melting solid's.** MSD = 0.0805
at t* = 10 is an rms displacement of 0.284 sigma, or 0.164 sigma per
axis, against a nearest-neighbour distance of 1.098 sigma. That is a
Lindemann ratio of **0.149** — the textbook criterion for a crystal at
its melting point is about 0.15. The system is a solid, right at the edge
of melting, and not crossing it.

**3. The VACF backscatters and then dies.** Minimum −0.303 at t* = 0.20,
decaying to 0.0009 by t* = 10. A backscattering minimum alone does not
distinguish a liquid from a solid — an atom rattling in a cage and an
atom rattling in a lattice both reverse. Combined with a flat MSD it is
unambiguous: the reversals never add up to transport.

**4. g(r) keeps long-range order the reference liquid has lost.** This is
where an earlier version of this investigation got it wrong, and the
correction matters. The previous write-up said g(r) showed "an
oscillation regrowing rather than damping out". It does not. The
principal peaks decay monotonically:

    3.125 (r* = 1.098)  ->  1.531 (r* = 2.004)  ->  1.318 (r* = 3.051)

The true statement is stronger than the false one. Measured against
experiment 03's Rahman g(r) — identical code, identical normalisation,
lower density rho* = 0.8177, a genuine liquid — the envelope damps far
more slowly:

| | ours (rho* = 0.900) | reference (rho* = 0.8177) |
|---|---|---|
| principal peaks | 3.125, 1.531, 1.318 | 2.841, 1.258, 1.087 |
| \|g−1\| at the 3rd peak | **0.318** | **0.087** |
| g(r) at the far end | 1.102 at r* = 4.02 | 0.981–1.015 over r* = 4.5–5.0 |

The third peak stands **3.7x** further from the bulk value g = 1 than
the reference liquid's does, and where the reference has settled to
within 2% of 1, ours is still 10% above it at the edge of the accessible
range.

There is also a feature the reference simply does not have. Between the
second and third peaks our g(r) has **two** minima, at r* = 2.367
(g = 0.846) and r* = 2.789 (g = 0.812), separated by a sub-unity local
maximum at r* = 2.588 (g = 0.960) — a split shoulder. The reference has a
single smooth minimum at r* = 2.533 (g = 0.853). A split second-shell
shoulder is a crystalline signature: it is the fcc lattice's distinct
neighbour distances resolving themselves where a liquid's would be
smeared into one broad trough. All three seeds show it at the same
positions.

## Why this happens

`moldyn_run` always starts from a *perfect* fcc lattice and equilibrates
directly at the target temperature. At rho* = 0.900 and T* = 0.851 the
system sits close enough to the Lennard-Jones freezing line that the
perfect lattice is at least metastable, and 50 t* of Langevin at the
target temperature never supplies the fluctuation needed to break it. The
five lower densities are further from the line, melt within the same 50
t*, and agree with NIST to a few parts in a thousand.

Both signs of the disagreement follow directly:

- **U* too negative by 0.390.** An ordered lattice sits deeper in the
  pair potential than a disordered liquid at the same density. More
  cohesive is exactly what "did not melt" predicts.
- **p* too low by 2.452.** At fixed density an ordered packing relieves
  the short-range repulsion that dominates the virial at rho* = 0.900.
  NIST's liquid is strongly compressed at 2.544; our lattice is nearly
  stress-free at 0.092.

A units or convention error is ruled out: the other five densities use
the identical code path and agree, and experiment 01 independently
reproduces all twelve NIST energy/virial reference values to within
NIST's own published precision. A temperature-offset artefact is ruled
out: |dT*| at this row is 0.0018.

## A correction to the previous conclusion

This note supersedes an earlier reading of the same state point, and the
change is worth recording rather than quietly overwriting.

The previous diagnostic ran **one** trajectory and measured
D_msd = 0.0203 — suppressed relative to NIST's 0.027 but clearly nonzero.
It concluded "not a rigid, zero-diffusion crystal, but not a
fully-equilibrated liquid either — partially ordered and still mobile".

That conclusion was wrong, and it was wrong for an instructive reason.
About 0.0046 of that 0.0203 was not diffusion at all: it was the
centre-of-mass drift described in
[2026-09-21-exp04-einstein-vs-green-kubo.md](2026-09-21-exp04-einstein-vs-green-kubo.md),
which adds `<v_com^2> * t_mid / 3` to any Einstein estimate. With the
drift removed the same seed gives 1.5e-5. A spurious drift had made a
frozen crystal look like a slow liquid — and the single-trajectory
diagnostic had no way to notice.

Two things changed as a result. The drift is fixed at the source. And
this experiment now runs three seeds rather than one: a state point whose
answer flips between two last-bit-different trajectories is a state point
one trajectory cannot characterise. The threshold itself was not touched.

## What would fix it, and why it was not done here

Melting a near-close-packed lattice this close to the freezing line needs
a protocol the driver does not have: an explicit melt-then-quench stage
(equilibrate well above the target temperature, then cool to it), or a
disordered or liquid-configuration start instead of a perfect lattice.
Either is a change to how every simulation in the project is initialised,
and neither is in this phase's scope. The disagreement is therefore
reported as it stands, with the mechanism identified, rather than
patched or absorbed into a wider tolerance.

Two smaller limitations for whoever picks this up:

- The Green-Kubo window is capped at t* = 10 by `kMaxLagCap = 2000` in
  `apps/moldyn_run.cpp`, 10% of the 100 t* trajectory. That does not
  matter for the conclusion here (both estimators agree on
  "indistinguishable from zero"), but it would matter for measuring a
  real D at this density. Raising it is not a config change: `Msd`/`Vacf`
  accumulation is O(frames x maxLag x natoms), and a t* = 50 window at
  the current sampling rate is roughly 1e11 operations. Extending the
  correlation window properly needs a frame-sampling stride, which is
  new driver scope, recorded for Phase 3's performance pass.
- g(r) is only tabulated out to r* = 4.02 (just under half the box
  length, the hard limit `Rdf::accumulate` enforces at N = 500). The
  reference comparison at r* = 4.5–5.0 comes from experiment 03's larger
  N = 864 box.
